# Autocatalysis

Max for Live instrument (`device/Autocatalysis.amxd`). It plays guitar-into-amp feedback: MIDI notes
pluck six virtual strings, two virtual amps play them back into the room, and the room sound drives
the strings again. Hold a note and it blooms into feedback on one of its harmonics. Which harmonic
wins depends on how far the amps are, how the strings ring and how hard the amps are pushed, the same
way it does on stage.

The name is the dynamics. The feedback feeds itself until it runs out of supply: the amp's power
supply sags under load, and a reaction term (a Brusselator, the textbook autocatalytic oscillator)
modulates the power stage. Push Catalysis past its threshold and the amp breathes.

Original design. No sampled amps or impulse responses, and no claim to model any specific amp.

## Signal path

```
MIDI ─► 6 waveguide strings ─► neck pickup ─► amp L ─┬─► out L
            ▲   ▲               bridge pickup ─► amp R ─┼─► out R
            │   └── body (solid → hollow) ◄──┐          │
            └──────── air drive ◄──────────── air ◄─────┘
                                     (L at Distance, R at Distance + Offset, Stage reflections)
```

All of this runs in one gen~, one sample at a time, so the loop delay is the real air delay
(Distance / 343 m/s) and nothing else. The GenExpr is generated by `scripts/dsp.py`.

- **Strings.** Six Karplus-Strong waveguides with a one-pole loop filter (Damp). The loop length is
  corrected for the filter's delay, so tuning holds. A pluck is one period of filtered noise (Pick,
  velocity). A held string rings for Sustain (T60). A released one is damped to about 70 ms unless
  Ring or the sustain pedal is on. Before you play, the strings sit at standard tuning (E A D G B E).
- **Air into the strings.** The drive into each string is scaled by what that string loses per period
  when it is free to ring, raised to the 0.7 power. So a held string's resonance peak grows a little
  with sustain and pitch, and a damped string passes about 35 dB less. A released string also has its
  coupling cut by a further 24 dB, because fingers on a string soak up the drive. A one-pole low-pass
  at 1.5 kHz stands in for air pressure moving a string less at high frequencies. Feedback is the
  master gain for the whole loop.
- **Pickups.** Each string has a neck tap (position 0.25 of the scale) and a bridge tap (0.065). A
  tap is a comb, `out − 0.85·delayed` with the delay at β·(string period). That puts the notches
  where a real pickup has them: the neck nulls harmonics 4, 8, …, the bridge about every 15th.
  Coil/cable resonances
  sit at 3.2 kHz (neck) and 4.6 kHz (bridge). The neck pickup feeds amp L and the bridge pickup feeds
  amp R. **Spread** blends both toward the middle (0 % = mono).
- **Body.** Two resonances of the air arriving at the guitar, added to the pickups. Body 0 % is a
  solid body: a stiff 180 Hz mode, barely coupled. 100 % is a hollow body: loose 98 / 203 Hz modes,
  strongly coupled. It can howl on its own, which is what hollow bodies do.
- **Amp (×2).** 90 Hz high-pass, then **Drive**. The preamp is a tanh with **Bias** offset (even
  harmonics), anti-aliased with first-order ADAA (antiderivative of log cosh). The DC point goes
  through the same ADAA so silence stays exactly zero. Next is a **Tone** tilt around 650 Hz, then
  the power stage, a second ADAA tanh. **Cab** is 1x12 open, 2x12 or 4x12 closed: a high-pass, a
  low resonance bump, a presence peak and a low-pass, all TPT state-variable filters.
- **Supply (Sag / Recover).** Reservoir S per amp, 0..1. Output power burns it (Sag) and it refills
  toward 1 at 1/Recover. Power gain is proportional to S², so the feedback compresses and blooms. The
  face shows S as the two Supply meters.
- **Reaction (Catalysis / Rate).** A Brusselator per amp,
  `X' = A − (B+1)X + X²Y`, `Y' = BX − X²Y`, with B = 5 · Catalysis, A = 1 + 0.6 · the amp's own
  output level, and time scaled by Rate. The power stage is multiplied by X/A, which is 1 at rest.
  - Below B = 1 + A² it settles. After a bloom it rings briefly, then holds.
  - Above that, X runs a limit cycle and the amp breathes: a slow sag, then a sharp swell.
  - Loud feedback raises A and pushes the threshold up. So the amp pulses near the edge and holds
    when it screams. The two amps see different levels, so they drift out of step and the breathing
    goes stereo.
  - The Kinetics display plots X (right) against Y (up) for each amp. A still dot is a steady amp; a
    loop is a breathing one.
- **Air.** **Distance** 0.1–8 m sets each amp's delay to the guitar and its inverse-distance level.
  High-frequency loss grows with distance. Amp R sits **Offset** metres further back. **Stage** adds
  two reflections per amp, at different path lengths for L and R. Distance changes glide over about
  80 ms, so sweeping it bends pitch a little, like walking.
- **Seek.** Works out where to stand instead of hunting for it.
  - Every 256 samples it computes the open-loop response of the whole feedback path at the last
    note's harmonics 1–6. That covers both air paths and their reflections, the amp and cab
    filters, the pickup combs and coils, Spread, and the strings' resonance at each harmonic. All of
    it comes from the live coefficients.
  - A harmonic sustains where the loop phase is zero. So the air-delay offsets that put the target
    (**Fund** / **Oct** / **12th** / **2 Oct**) there are known exactly, and inside one fundamental
    period there are as many of them as the harmonic number.
  - Seek ranks those spots by how strongly they line up the competing harmonics and glides there
    (0.25 s). The spot follows Distance, Offset, Spread, Cab and Tone as you move them.
  - If the target hasn't taken over 3 s after arriving, it tries the next spot. Once the target has
    held a spot for half a second, Seek stays there even if another note takes over later, so it
    doesn't wander.
  - The seek strip shows Seeking / Locked / Weak and the offset in degrees of the fundamental.
    **Weak** means the other harmonics out-gain the target even at its best spot. Examples are the
    low-E fundamental, which sits under the amp's 90 Hz high-pass and the cab's, and the 4th
    harmonic, which the neck pickup nulls.
  - Turning Seek off eases the offset back to zero.
- **Output.** The two cab outputs (close-miked), DC-blocked, through **Output** and a tanh ceiling at
  0.97.

## Playing

- Notes take strings in this order:
  1. a string already on that pitch (re-pluck);
  2. otherwise the free string released longest ago;
  3. otherwise the oldest held string.

  Up to six notes sound at once. Released strings keep their pitch.
- **Sustain pedal** (CC 64) acts as Ring while it is down. Rung strings stay coupled, so open strings
  and old notes join the feedback.
- **Pitch bend** ±2 semitones bends all strings. **All Notes Off** (CC 123, sent when the transport
  stops) releases everything.
- **Mute** damps every string and closes the loop within about 20 ms. Use it as the panic button.
- MIDI never writes a Live parameter, so playing leaves undo and automation alone.

Where to start:
- Hold a low note.
- Raise **Feedback** until it blooms. At the default Drive of 20 dB that happens around 25–35 %.
- Sweep **Distance** to make it jump between harmonics. Sweeping toward the amp gives the classic
  stepping-closer sound.
- Turn **Catalysis** up past about 50 % with **Rate** 0.5–2 Hz to make it breathe.

## Controls

| Section | Control | Range | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Strings | Sustain | 0.2–60 s | Ring time (T60) of a held string |
| | Pick | 0–100 % | Pluck brightness |
| | Damp | 0–100 % | String loop loss. Higher pushes the feedback toward lower harmonics |
| | Body | 0–100 % | Solid → hollow body |
| | Ring | toggle | Released strings keep ringing and stay coupled (same as the sustain pedal) |
| | Mute | toggle | Damp all strings and close the loop |
| Amp | Drive | 0–40 dB | Preamp gain. The main "how easily it feeds back" control besides Feedback |
| | Bias | ±100 % | Preamp asymmetry (even harmonics) |
| | Tone | ±100 % | Tilt EQ, ±8 dB |
| | Spread | 0–100 % | Neck → L / bridge → R split. 0 % = both amps get the mix |
| | Cab | 1x12 / 2x12 / 4x12 | Cabinet voicing |
| Kinetics | Sag | 0–100 % | How hard output power drains the supply |
| | Recover | 10–3000 ms | Supply refill time |
| | Catalysis | 0–100 % | Reaction strength. Above about 40 % (quiet) to 65 % (loud) the amp breathes |
| | Rate | 0.05–8 Hz | Reaction speed (roughly cycles per second) |
| Space | Distance | 0.1–8 m | Guitar-to-amp distance |
| | Offset | 0–4 m | How much further back amp R stands |
| | Stage | 0–100 % | Room reflections |
| | Seek | toggle | Move the air delay to where the selected harmonic wins |
| | Harmonic | Fund / Oct / 12th / 2 Oct | Seek target |
| Output | Feedback | 0–100 % | Loop gain (squared taper) |
| | Output | −70 to +6 dB | Level |

Live banks: **Play** (Feedback, Drive, Distance, Offset, Sustain, Damp, Body, Output) ·
**Kinetics** (Sag, Recover, Catalysis, Rate, Bias, Tone, Spread, Stage) ·
**Switches** (Pick, Cab, Ring, Mute, Seek, Harmonic).

## Build and test

```
cd autocatalysis
python3 scripts/build.py                 # device/Autocatalysis.amxd (frozen) + editable .maxpat, loose JS, .genexpr
node --test tests/dsp.test.cjs tests/js.test.cjs
node tests/render.cjs                    # offline audition WAVs in tests/renders/ (harness, not native)
python3 scripts/native_qa.py             # device/qa/Autocatalysis QA.maxpat: open it in Max, wait for DONE
python3 scripts/check_native.py          # analyse the native recordings + console log
```

- `tests/harness.cjs` translates the generated GenExpr to JS and runs it offline. Its noise is a
  seeded PRNG and delay reads are linear, the same as the device's `interp="linear"`.
- The native QA runs the real gen~ in Max and records eleven cases: silence, dry pluck, bloom,
  release, mute, breathing, seek off / on / octave, mono, extreme chord.
  - It switches Max to the NonRealTime driver at 48 kHz, with the scheduler in audio interrupt, so
    it needs no audio interface and the case timers follow audio time.
  - It saves the previous driver, sample rate, overdrive and interrupt settings and restores them at
    the end; the `RESTORED` line in `native.log` shows what it put back.

## Known limits

- One string model for every note: no fret position, no string-specific inharmonicity.
- The cabinet is four filters, not an impulse response. Close-miked only: no room mic output.
- The two amps share the strings. So the stereo comes from different pickups, distances and supply
  and reaction states, not from independent feedback pitches. Expect width, not two different notes.
- With Feedback and Drive both near maximum, damped strings and the body can still keep a squeal
  going after release. That happens with a real amp too.
